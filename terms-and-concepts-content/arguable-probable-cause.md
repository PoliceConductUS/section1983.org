## What It Means

Arguable probable cause describes the room for reasonable error that [qualified immunity](/terms-and-concepts/qualified-immunity) gives an officer deciding whether to arrest you. The defense may obtain immunity even if actual [probable cause](/terms-and-concepts/probable-cause) was absent, when a reasonable officer could have believed the known facts justified the arrest.

This is a defense to the officer's civil liability. The constitutional requirement for probable cause and the separate immunity question both need attention. Courts use different formulations, so check the controlling cases in your circuit.

## What You Should Be Able to Distinguish

For a false-arrest claim, separate these questions:

1. Did the facts known at the time establish probable cause for an offense?
2. If they fell short, was the officer's mistake reasonable under the law that applied then?

The judge may resolve the case on immunity without deciding that the arrest was constitutional. Your record should address both the claimed basis for arrest and why the asserted mistake was unreasonable.

## A Concrete Example

In [Club Retro, L.L.C. v. Hilton](/cases/club-retro-v-hilton), the Fifth Circuit allowed false-arrest claims to proceed beyond a motion to dismiss. The allegations supported an inference that deputies learned their asserted factual justification after arresting the nightclub owners. Later discoveries could not supply the missing basis for the earlier arrests.

The court called arguable probable cause reasonable but mistaken probable cause. It accepted the pleaded facts for that appeal; the ruling allowed the claims to continue rather than establishing final liability. [Read the opinion, page 33](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/08/08-30512-CV0.wpd.pdf).

## What the Defense Will Say

The defense will emphasize what officers knew, what witnesses reported, and the offense those facts appeared to support. It may argue that an error was reasonable even if the charge later failed.

Build a timeline with three columns: the fact claimed, when the officer learned it, and the record supporting or disputing it. Then compare the known facts with the offense's requirements and the controlling cases. A later dismissal or acquittal leaves the arrest-time inquiry to be answered.

At the pleading stage, explain the facts in your complaint. At [summary judgment](/terms-and-concepts/summary-judgment), identify the admissible evidence supporting your account and the material factual disputes.
