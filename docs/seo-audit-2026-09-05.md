# SEO Audit: www.section1983.org

Date: 2026-09-05
Scope: full site audit (technical, on-page, content gaps, competitors)
Data sources: local build (434 pages), live HTTP checks, Lighthouse 12 (mobile, headless Chrome), web search for SERP composition. Ahrefs and Similarweb were not connected, so difficulty and volume are estimates from SERP composition, not tool data. Google Analytics was not firing before this date, so there is no traffic baseline.

## Executive summary

The site has a strong foundation: 434 indexable pages, roughly 315,000 words of original content, one H1 per page, alt text on every image, rich structured data on every template, and a Lighthouse mobile score of 96 performance and 100 SEO. Its biggest strength is depth in a niche that the large legal publishers cover only with single thin explainers.

The three priorities that will move rankings most:

1. Fix the duplicate-host problem. The apex domain and www both serve 200 responses, and Google has indexed both.
2. Rewrite title tags so they contain the words people search. Use "Section 1983" rather than "§ 1983", drop the "Case Law Library" filler, and add topic and citation to case titles.
3. Turn the 252 case pages from dead ends into an internal-linking engine with related terms and articles, plus crawlable state and circuit hub pages.

Overall assessment: strong foundation, needs work on titles, duplication, and hub structure.

## Content gap recommendations

- **State statute of limitations pages.** High priority, moderate effort. No one owns this query set; law reviews and a 2012 Ninth Circuit outline rank. One table page plus 50 short state pages linking into the accrual and tolling terms.
- **State and circuit hub pages.** High priority, moderate effort. "Fifth Circuit qualified immunity cases" has a weak SERP of law-firm blogs. Build hubs from existing tags.
- **Downloadable sample complaint.** High priority, quick win. The Jailhouse Lawyers Handbook and ezel.ai rank with weaker material. Package the existing sample complaint and declaration as PDFs on a dedicated page.
- **"How to sue the police" pillar.** High priority, substantial. Nolo, FindLaw, and LawInfo hold this with 800-word explainers. A 3,000-word pillar routing to the 13 process steps can compete on depth.
- **Monell pillar.** Medium priority, moderate effort. Seven Monell articles with no parent page dilute each other.
- **Timeline and money questions.** Medium priority, quick win. "How long does it take" and "settlement amounts" have thin SERPs. FAQ schema fits.
- **Freshness.** Set a quarterly review cadence and update `updatedDate` only when substance changes.

## Prioritized action plan

### Quick wins (this week)

1. Redirect apex to www in the CloudFront function. High impact, 1 hour, needs a Terraform apply.
2. Add a 404 page. High impact, 30 minutes.
3. Retitle articles and terms to use "Section 1983" and add context to short titles. High impact, 2 hours.
4. Case title pattern with citation and topic tags; drop the suffix. High impact, 2 hours.
5. Trim 23 long descriptions. Medium impact, 1 hour.
6. Fix sitemap `lastmod` to use real content dates. Medium impact, 1 hour.
7. Set `Cache-Control` on `_astro/` in the deploy workflow. Low SEO impact, 15 minutes.
8. Verify Search Console and submit the sitemap. Prerequisite for everything else.

### Strategic investments (this quarter)

1. Related content block on case pages driven by tags. High impact, half day.
2. State and circuit hub pages as static routes. High impact, one to two days. Depends on the tag mapping.
3. Statute of limitations by state table plus pages. High impact, two to three days of legal research.
4. Downloadable sample complaint and declaration with a landing page. High impact, half day.
5. "How to sue the police" pillar and a Monell pillar. High impact, multi-day each.
6. Schema cleanup for HowTo, LegalCase, and author typing. Low impact, half day.
7. Homepage copy with keyword-bearing H2s. Medium impact, 2 hours.
