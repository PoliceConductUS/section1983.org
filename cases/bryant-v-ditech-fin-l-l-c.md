## What This Case Is About

[Bryant](/cases/bryant-v-ditech-fin-l-l-c) illustrates how factual allegations can support a reasonable inference at the complaint stage. The dispute concerned ownership of a condominium, and the Fifth Circuit applied the ordinary federal [pleading standard](/terms-and-concepts/plausibility-pleading). It reversed dismissal and allowed the claim to proceed.

## The Facts

Khaliq Bryant acquired a Dallas condominium after a series of transfers. A mortgage lender asserted a right to foreclose based on an earlier owner's loan. Bryant brought a quiet-title action, asking the court to resolve the lender's competing claim against the property.

Bryant alleged that the loan had been accelerated more than four years before suit. **Acceleration** means the lender declares the remaining debt due at once. That date mattered to the Texas time limit on foreclosure. The lender argued that the complaint inadequately specified the required acceleration notices.

## What the Court Decided

The court reviewed the dismissal independently. It concluded that the alleged prolonged nonpayment and repeated property transfers supported a reasonable inference that acceleration had occurred outside the limitations period. The complaint could proceed despite omitting the notice dates. [Read the opinion, pages 3–6](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/opinions/unpub/23/23-10416.0.pdf).

The ruling concerned what could reasonably be inferred from the pleaded facts. Default or a property transfer alone does not automatically establish acceleration, and the court had yet to decide whether Bryant could prove his allegations. The opinion is unpublished.

## What the Judge and Defense Will Examine

For your § 1983 complaint, identify the factual allegations supporting each necessary inference. The defense will argue that a missing fact leaves only speculation. Explain how the facts you do allege support the conclusion the judge must evaluate.

[Twombly](/cases/bell-atlantic-corp-v-twombly) and [Iqbal](/cases/ashcroft-v-iqbal) require a plausible factual basis. At this stage, the judge generally accepts well-pleaded facts and reasonable inferences while evaluating whether they support a claim. Proof becomes important at later stages.

## How to Use the Decision

Compare the allegation, the facts supporting it, and the inference you want the judge to draw. [Bryant](/cases/bryant-v-ditech-fin-l-l-c) offers a pleading example from a property dispute; use civil-rights authorities to establish the requirements of your particular § 1983 claim.
