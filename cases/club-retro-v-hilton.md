## What Happened

A nightclub and its owners sued Rapides Parish Sheriff William Hilton and deputies after a raid, searches, seizures, and arrests. Their complaint described a forceful operation that went beyond the regulatory inspections the defendants invoked to justify it.

The defendants appealed a ruling denying [qualified immunity](/terms-and-concepts/qualified-immunity) on several claims. The Fifth Circuit reviewed the sufficiency of the complaint, accepting the properly pleaded facts for that review. The decision was issued May 6, 2009, and revised June 5, 2009. [Read the official opinion](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/08/08-30512-CV0.wpd.pdf).

## What the Court Decided

The Fourth Amendment search, seizure, and false-arrest claims could proceed beyond the immunity motion. For the arrests, the allegations supported an inference that deputies learned their asserted factual justification only after arresting the owners. The court explained that even [arguable probable cause](/terms-and-concepts/arguable-probable-cause)—a reasonable but mistaken belief that probable cause exists—must rest on the information available when the arrest occurs. See opinion page 33.

The defendants obtained immunity on the First Amendment and equal-protection claims. The court also dismissed the part of the appeal concerning due-process claims that the district court had dismissed without prejudice. It remanded for further proceedings. See opinion pages 47–48.

## How to Use the Decision

For an arrest claim, separate facts known before the arrest from facts discovered afterward. For a search claim, identify the authority the defense invokes and compare its limits with what the officers allegedly did.

The defense may describe a mistake as reasonable or characterize a search as a permitted inspection. Your complaint should explain the specific facts that make those descriptions inadequate. [Club Retro](/cases/club-retro-v-hilton) illustrates how allegations can defeat an early immunity motion; proving those allegations remains a later task.

## Limits of the Decision

The ruling produced different outcomes for different claims. Treat the Fourth Amendment analysis as a pleading-stage decision under the law governing that encounter, and research the controlling cases for your own facts and date.
